\section{Goals and Motivation}

\begin{quote}
    \enquote{Information is a source of learning. But unless it is organized, processed, and available to the right people in a format for decision making, it is a burden, not a benefit.} \\
    \citet{Pollard}
\end{quote}

Technology and innovation are key drivers of economic prosperity, the standard of living, and societal progress.
They fuel economic growth by enabling businesses and governments to optimize their operations, increase productivity,
and create new opportunities in an increasingly interconnected global market.
For individuals, technological progress translates into better healthcare, improved education, and more convenient
everyday solutions, while breakthroughs in renewable energy, medical research, and communication systems help address
global challenges such as climate change, public health, and social inequality.
In light of growing geopolitical tensions and global competition, technological capabilities have also become a matter of
industrial competitiveness, technological sovereignty, and resilience.
Austria's Industrial Strategy 2035 \todo{cite IS2} responds to this by placing a selected set of key technology fields at
the centre of its future industrial and innovation policy.
Rather than creating an entirely new funding system, it builds on the existing innovation infrastructure, in which
funding agencies such as the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) and the Austrian Research Promotion Agency (FFG)
\todo{cite FWF, FFG} already manage extensive portfolios of research and innovation projects, ranging from basic research
to application-oriented development and industrial implementation.

However, steering such a portfolio is nearly impossible without access to accurate and comprehensive data.
Data provides the foundation for identifying strengths and gaps, measuring performance, and uncovering opportunities for
advancement; without it, any attempt to prioritize technologies or allocate funding would be like navigating in the dark.
Yet, as the quote above suggests, the mere availability of data is not sufficient.
Policymakers, analysts, and funding agencies typically operate under time pressure and are rarely data specialists
themselves.
Information that is scattered across sources, inconsistently structured, or only accessible through raw tables and
lengthy reports places the burden of collecting, cleaning, and interpreting it on the decision-maker, which in practice
means that it is often not used at all.
Only when data is well structured, consistently linked, and presented in an intuitive and explorable way can it be
turned into actionable insight.
Well-designed visualizations and interactive dashboards allow users to grasp the overall picture at a glance, to drill
down into the details where needed, and to discover relationships that would remain hidden in a textual summary.
In this way, they lower the barrier to evidence-based policymaking and enable more transparent, informed, and
impact-oriented decisions.

The TechAtlas platform addresses this need for the Austrian research and innovation landscape.
By integrating project, organization, and funding data from the FWF and the FFG into a single analytical environment,
it enables key users, such as politicians, policymakers, funding agencies, and analysts, to explore technology-specific
project portfolios, collaboration networks between academia and industry, and the geographical distribution of research
and innovation activities across Austria's key technology fields.
It thereby provides a transparent evidence base for identifying thematic concentrations, institutional linkages,
regional patterns, and potential gaps in strategic technology portfolios, and supports aligning publicly funded research
with the strategic priorities of the Industrial Strategy 2035.

An intuitive dashboard, however, can only be as good as the data underneath it.
This thesis therefore focuses on the data foundation of TechAtlas.
It aims to develop a configurable data pipeline that gathers heterogeneous project, organization, and funding data from
multiple sources, resolves inconsistencies between them, enriches them with additional publicly available information,
and combines them into a consistent, continuously updated dataset.
Since data sources and their interfaces change over time, the pipeline is designed to be configured, monitored, and
maintained by domain experts without developer support, ensuring that TechAtlas remains a reliable and up-to-date
evidence base in the long term.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\todo{Data needed is scattered, inconsistent and changes}

While several existing tools exist to catalogue and evaluate the current innovation landscape across the key technologies in Austria, none of them can yet give a full picture linking all the projects, organizations, and grants together into a single cohesive navigable dataset.
The websites of the funding institution themselves have a repository only of their funded projects. This leaves the data fragmented across many providers making a unified search almost impossible to perform in a timely manner.
While other governmentally issued or commissioned reports attempt to solve this, they always just represent a snapshot in time, instead of a continuously updating dataset.
They also give their findings as a textual report of the current situation, instead of the explorable data set we aim to create here.

Despite the complementary strengths of these solutions, we found the current situation of data concerning the development and research of key technologies in Austria and the cooperations between research institutions and private companies to be fragmented, unintuitive and unable to show the cooperative connections we were looking for.

\section{Research Questions}

Main: How can a configurable data pipeline combine heterogeneous research-funding data into a consistent, enriched
dataset that non-developers can maintain and adapt to change?

Data generation: What data quality problems arise when combining FFG and FWF data on projects, organizations and
funding, and how can they be overcome with publicly available data?

Architecture: Which architecture lets data pipelines be configured, scheduled, monitored and versioned through a web
interface?

Adaptability and usability: To what extent can domain experts run the pipeline, publish datasets and configure it to
changing source APIs without developer support?
